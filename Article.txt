Shashank Koirala may have harboured the ambition of leading the Nepali Congress as its president, but the former general secretary now stands at a historic crossroads. He is preparing to lead a prospective new political outfit outside the traditional four-starred banner and election symbol “tree”.
Following opposition from the Sher Bahadur Deuba-led faction to the Congress’s special general convention held earlier this year, the group is organising a three-day national gathering in Kathmandu from August 14 to 16. It will prepare for the formation of a separate political party, unless a dramatic reconciliation occurs between the Congress factions anytime soon.
When the former acting president Purna Bahadur Khadka stepped back from coordinating the event, the Deuba faction appointed Koirala on Saturday to steer the 521-member main organising committee. Until then, Khadka had been heading the activities of the Deuba faction.
On Saturday, Shashank not only replaced Khadka but also took on the leadership role with a firm commitment to spearheading a new party—minus the tree symbol and the four-starred flag.
“Shashank Koirala is our leader today, and he will remain our leader moving forward,” said senior party leader Bimalendra Nidhi.
The day after his appointment, Shashank hosted the first top-level strategic meeting at his residence in Maharajgunj on Sunday. However, his decision has drawn sharp criticism across social media platforms, with many accusing the son of the legendary founder BP Koirala, of engineering a party split. Public sentiment remains largely hostile to the move.
Muktinath Panta, a former security supervisor currently employed at the Smithsonian Institution’s National Air and Space Museum, expressed deep dismay over Shashank’s decision on Facebook. “My goodness, what has happened to the intellect of BP’s son? Where will following leaders like Mohan Bahadur Basnet, Min Bishwakarma, Kanchharam Tamang, and Kundan Raj Kafle lead him after breaking the party? Destruction leads to perverted wisdom. What would BP think from above? He could have at least remained inactive. After enjoying power for so many years on his father’s legacy, he is now choosing to sink the ship along with himself.”
